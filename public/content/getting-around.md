# Getting Around: Transportation in the Continent-Sized Country

Brazil is the fifth-largest country in the world, making transportation planning a critical part of your trip. You can't just "drive from Rio to the Amazon"—that's a 2,500-mile journey. Here’s how to navigate the options.

### Domestic Flights: The Primary Connector
For travel between major regions, flying is the only practical option.
- **The "Big Three" Airlines**: **LATAM**, **GOL**, and **Azul**. 
    - **Azul** is often ranked highest for service and has a major hub in Campinas (VCP), which serves the São Paulo area.
    - **LATAM** and **GOL** are the major players for routes between Rio (GIG/SDU) and São Paulo (GRU/CGH).
- **The "Ponte Aérea"**: The Rio-São Paulo air bridge is one of the busiest in the world. For the best views and most convenience, try to fly into **Santos Dumont (SDU)** in Rio and **Congonhas (CGH)** in São Paulo—both are located right in the city centers.
- **Booking Tip**: Use Google Flights to compare, but sometimes booking directly on the Portuguese version of the airline site (using a VPN) can reveal lower "resident" prices, though this is becoming less common.

### Intercity Buses (Ônibus)
Brazilians use long-distance buses far more than Americans use Greyhound. The system is clean, safe, and often luxurious.
- **Classes of Service**: 
    - **Convencional**: Standard bus.
    - **Executivo**: More legroom.
    - **Leito**: The "gold standard." These seats reclines almost 180 degrees into a bed. It’s often better than a domestic flight for 6–10 hour journeys.
- **Booking**: Use sites like **BuscaOnibus** or **ClickBus**. You will need your passport number (or CPF) to buy a ticket.
- **Buser**: This is the "Uber of buses." It’s often 50% cheaper than traditional lines and uses high-quality private charters. Download the app.

### Ride-Sharing: Uber & 99
In cities, do not bother with traditional street taxis unless you are at a designated airport stand.
- **Uber**: Works exactly like in the US. It is very cheap by American standards. A 20-minute ride in Rio might only cost $5 USD.
- **99 (pronounced "Meia-Meia" or "Noventa e Nove")**: This is the local competitor. It often has more drivers than Uber in certain cities and can be slightly cheaper.
- **Safety**: Always check the license plate. In high-traffic areas, drivers may ask for a "PIN" to start the ride—this is a great safety feature to ensure you’re in the right car.

### Metro Systems
- **São Paulo**: The Metro is world-class, clean, and very safe during the day. It is the best way to avoid SP’s legendary traffic jams. Buy a "Bilhete Único" card at any station.
- **Rio de Janeiro**: The Metro is simpler (essentially two main lines) but very effective for getting from Ipanema/Copacabana to the Downtown (Centro) area.

### Rental Cars
Unless you are heading to the "Estrada Real" (historic Minas Gerais) or remote beach towns in the Northeast, **do not rent a car in the major cities.** 
- **Traffic**: Brazilian traffic is chaotic, and "creative" lane-splitting by motorcycles is the norm.
- **Parking**: Finding parking is a nightmare, and you will often encounter "Flanelinhas"—unofficial street parkers who ask for a small tip (R$5) to "watch" your car.
- **License**: Your valid US driver’s license is technically legal for 180 days, but having an International Driving Permit (IDP) from AAA is recommended to avoid hassles with highway police.

### The "Rodoviária" Warning
Every city has a major bus terminal called the *Rodoviária*. These are massive hubs. While the buses themselves are safe, the terminals can be magnets for pickpockets. Keep your bags between your legs and don't linger outside with your luggage.
