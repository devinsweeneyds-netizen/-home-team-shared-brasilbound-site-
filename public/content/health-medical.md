# Health & Medical: Staying Well in the Tropics

Brazil is home to some of the world's best hospitals, particularly in São Paulo, but it also presents specific tropical health risks that American travelers aren't always prepared for. Here is what you need to know to stay healthy and navigate the medical system.

### Recommended Vaccinations
While Brazil does not currently require proof of vaccination for US citizens (this changes periodically, so check the [CDC website](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/brazil) before you fly), the following are strongly recommended:
- **Yellow Fever**: Recommended for most people over 9 months old traveling to Brazil. Note that you should get this at least 10 days before your trip.
- **Hepatitis A & B**: Recommended for most travelers.
- **Typhoid**: Recommended if you are visiting smaller cities or rural areas.
- **Routine Vaccines**: Ensure you are up to date on MMR, Tetanus, and Flu shots.

### Mosquito-Borne Diseases
Mosquitoes are more than a nuisance in Brazil; they carry several significant viruses.
- **Dengue Fever**: This is the most common concern, especially during the rainy season (summer/fall). Symptoms include high fever, severe headache, and joint pain. 
- **Zika Virus**: If you are pregnant or planning to become pregnant, consult your doctor before traveling to Brazil due to the risk of birth defects.
- **Prevention**: Use a repellent with **25-50% DEET** or Picaridin. Wear long sleeves and pants in the evenings. Most high-end hotels use window screens or air conditioning to keep mosquitoes out, but smaller pousadas may not.

### The "Water" Rule
**Never drink the tap water.** 
- Even in big cities like Rio and São Paulo, the water is heavily chlorinated and the pipes are often old. 
- **Bottled Water**: Look for "Água Mineral" (Still) or "Água com Gás" (Sparkling).
- **Ice**: Ice in major hotels and reputable restaurants is usually made from filtered water and is safe. However, avoid ice in street drinks or from beach vendors if you have a sensitive stomach.

### Pharmacies (Farmácias)
Brazilian pharmacies are ubiquitous and excellent. You will see a "Droga Raia" or "Pague Menos" on almost every corner.
- **Over-the-Counter**: Many medications that require a prescription in the US (like certain antibiotics or high-strength painkillers) can sometimes be bought over-the-counter in Brazil, though laws are tightening.
- **Pharmacists**: They are highly trained and can often diagnose and treat minor ailments (like a stomach bug or skin rash) on the spot.

### The Healthcare System: SUS vs. Private
Brazil has a dual healthcare system.
- **SUS (Sistema Único de Saúde)**: This is the free, universal public system. In an absolute emergency, anyone (including tourists) can be treated for free at a public hospital. However, wait times are long and facilities can be basic.
- **Private Hospitals**: For anything non-critical, go to a private hospital. Hospitals like **Hospital Israelita Albert Einstein** or **Sírio-Libanês** in São Paulo are world-class—often better than many US hospitals.
- **Cost**: Be prepared to pay upfront with a credit card if you don't have a recognized international insurance plan. Even the "expensive" private hospitals are often much cheaper than US list prices for the same procedure.

### Travel Insurance
**Do not skip this.** 
- Your US insurance (including Medicare) almost certainly does not cover you in Brazil.
- Your policy should include **Medical Evacuation** (MedEvac). If you get seriously ill in the Amazon or a remote beach town, a private flight back to the US or São Paulo can cost $50,000+.
- Recommended providers for Americans: **Allianz Travel**, **World Nomads**, or **SafetyWing**.

### Prescription Medications
- Bring your medications in their original bottles with the US prescription label attached.
- Carry a note from your doctor for any controlled substances (like Adderall or strong pain meds).
- **WhatsApp**: Most private doctors in Brazil communicate via WhatsApp. If you need a local prescription, you can often arrange a "Tele-consulta" (video call) and receive a digital prescription on your phone.
