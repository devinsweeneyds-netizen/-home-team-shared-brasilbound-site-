# Packing Checklist: What to Bring to Brazil

Packing for Brazil can be tricky because the climate varies wildly between the tropical North and the temperate South. However, for most American travelers hitting the major cities and beaches, here is your essential checklist.

### The "Must-Haves" (Documents & Tech)
- [ ] **Passport**: With at least 6 months validity.
- [ ] **e-Visa Printout**: (If traveling after April 10, 2025).
- [ ] **Physical Copy of Travel Insurance**: With the emergency contact number highlighted.
- [ ] **Credit/Debit Cards**: At least two (one Visa, one Mastercard). No-foreign-transaction-fee cards are best.
- [ ] **Type N Power Adapter**: Most US plugs won't fit Brazilian outlets.
- [ ] **Power Bank**: Essential for long days of using GPS and taking photos.
- [ ] **Unlocked Smartphone**: To use with a local SIM or eSIM.

### Clothing: The "Casual & Comfortable" Rule
- [ ] **Light Fabrics**: Linen and cotton are your best friends. It can be extremely humid.
- [ ] **Beachwear**: Bring your favorite swimsuits, but wait to buy your flip-flops (**Havaianas**) in Brazil—they are cheaper and there are thousands of designs.
- [ ] **A "Nice" Outfit**: For upscale restaurants in São Paulo or Rio. Think "smart casual"—a polo and chinos for men, a sundress or nice blouse for women.
- [ ] **Light Jacket/Sweater**: Even in summer, the air conditioning in buses, malls, and planes can be freezing.
- [ ] **Sturdy Walking Shoes**: Sidewalks in Brazil (the famous "pedra portuguesa") are beautiful but often uneven and slippery when wet.

### Health & Toiletries
- [ ] **Sunscreen**: **This is expensive in Brazil.** Bring your favorite brand from the US.
- [ ] **Repellent with DEET/Picaridin**: Essential for protection against Dengue and Zika.
- [ ] **Prescription Meds**: In their original bottles.
- [ ] **First Aid Kit**: Include Pepto-Bismol, Imodium, and Advil/Tylenol.
- [ ] **Personal Hygiene**: Most US brands (Dove, Pantene, etc.) are available in Brazil, so don't overpack basics.

### Miscellaneous
- [ ] **Small Umbrella or Rain Poncho**: Summer storms in Brazil are short but intense.
- [ ] **Daypack with Anti-Theft Features**: Useful for keeping your belongings secure in crowded areas.
- [ ] **Ziploc Bags**: To keep your electronics dry during boat trips or sudden downpours.
- [ ] **A "Fake" Wallet**: (Optional) Some travelers carry a cheap wallet with a few small bills and expired cards to hand over in the unlikely event of a mugging.

### What to Leave at Home
- [ ] **Expensive Jewelry & Watches**: Don't make yourself a target.
- [ ] **Heavy Boots**: Unless you are doing serious jungle trekking.
- [ ] **Too Much Cash**: You can pay for almost everything by card; don't walk around with large amounts of USD or Reais.
- [ ] **High-End Designer Bags**: Keep it low-profile.

**Pro Packing Tip**: Leave some extra room in your suitcase. Between Brazilian coffee, artisanal cachaça, and leather goods, you'll likely want to bring some souvenirs home.
