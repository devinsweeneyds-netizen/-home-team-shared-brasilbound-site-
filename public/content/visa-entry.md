# Visa & Entry Requirements for US Citizens

Navigating the entry requirements for Brazil has historically been a bit of a moving target due to "reciprocity" policies—where Brazil matches the visa requirements the US imposes on Brazilians. As of mid-2024, here is the definitive guide for American travelers.

### The Current Visa Exemption (Until April 2025)
The most important piece of news for American tourists is that you **do not currently need a visa** for short-term tourism or business stays. The Brazilian government has postponed the reintroduction of visa requirements for citizens of the US, Canada, and Australia until **April 10, 2025**. 

If your arrival date is on or before April 9, 2025, you can enter with just your passport and stay for up to 90 days.

### The e-Visa Transition (Post-April 10, 2025)
If you are planning a trip that begins after April 10, 2025, you will need to apply for an **e-Visa**. 
- **Cost**: The fee is **$80.90 USD** per person.
- **Validity**: For US citizens, the visa is exceptionally generous—it is valid for **10 years** and allows for multiple entries.
- **Process**: The application is entirely online. You will need to upload a digital photo, a scan of your passport's biography page, and potentially a flight confirmation or proof of income (though requirements for the e-visa are typically streamlined).
- **Processing Time**: Expect a turnaround of 5–10 business days, but it is highly recommended to apply at least a month in advance.

### Passport Essentials
Regardless of the visa rules, your passport must meet these two non-negotiable criteria:
1.  **6-Month Validity**: Your passport must be valid for at least six months beyond your date of entry into Brazil. Airlines will deny boarding if you have less time remaining.
2.  **Blank Pages**: Ensure you have at least one or two completely blank "Visa" pages for entry and exit stamps.

### Customs and Arrival
Upon landing, you will pass through immigration (Polícia Federal). They rarely ask for extensive documentation from US tourists, but having a printout of your return flight and your first night's hotel address is a smart "just in case" measure.

**Customs (Receita Federal)**: Brazil has strict rules on electronics. While personal items (one phone, one laptop, one camera) are fine, bringing multiple brand-new items in original packaging can trigger heavy import taxes. If you are bringing expensive equipment for professional use, look into a "Carnet ATA" to avoid issues.

### The "90-Day" Rule
Tourists can stay in Brazil for 90 days per visit. You can request an extension for another 90 days at any local Federal Police office, for a maximum of 180 days in a rolling 12-month period. Note that overstaying results in a daily fine (roughly R$100 per day) payable upon exit or your next entry.

**Pro Tip**: Always check the [Official e-Visa Portal](https://brazil.vfsevisa.com/) for the most recent updates, as deadlines have been moved multiple times in the past.
