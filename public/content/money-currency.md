# Money & Currency: Navigating the Brazilian Financial System

Brazil has a sophisticated and modern banking system, but for an American traveler, there are specific quirks—especially the "PIX" revolution—that you need to understand before you land.

### The Currency: Brazilian Real (BRL)
The official currency is the **Real** (plural: *Reais*). It is symbolized by **R$**.
- **Denominations**: Banknotes come in 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, and a rare 200. Coins are 5, 10, 25, 50 cents and 1 Real.
- **Exchange Rate**: For the last several years, the US Dollar has been strong against the Real, typically fluctuating between R$4.80 and R$5.50 to $1 USD. This makes Brazil an affordable destination for Americans.

### The PIX Revolution
You will see QR codes on every street cart, beach umbrella, and store counter. This is **PIX**, Brazil's instant payment system. 
- **The Catch for Americans**: Currently, you generally need a Brazilian bank account and a CPF (tax ID) to use PIX directly. 
- **The Workaround**: While you can't use the PIX app yourself, the fact that everyone uses it means many small vendors have stopped carrying much cash for change (*troco*). Always carry small bills (R$2, 5, 10) for situations where your card isn't accepted and they can't make change.

### Credit Cards & Apple/Google Pay
Brazil is ahead of the US in "tap-to-pay" technology. 
- **Acceptance**: Visa and Mastercard are accepted almost everywhere—from high-end restaurants to fruit vendors on the beach. American Express is accepted in major hotels and malls but is less common in smaller shops.
- **Fees**: Always choose to be charged in **BRL (local currency)** if the card terminal asks. Letting the terminal do the conversion (Dynamic Currency Conversion) always results in a worse rate.
- **Safety**: Never let your card leave your sight. In restaurants, the waiter will bring the "maquininha" (machine) to your table.

### ATM Tips (Saques)
Getting cash from an ATM can be hit-or-miss with US cards.
- **Reliable Banks**: **Banco do Brasil**, **Bradesco**, and **Santander** are usually the best for foreign cards. Avoid the red "24 Horas" ATMs if possible, as they charge high per-transaction fees (often R$25 or more).
- **Limits**: Most ATMs have a withdrawal limit after 10:00 PM for safety reasons (usually R$300–500). Do your banking during the day.
- **Travel Notice**: **Call your bank before you leave.** Brazil is often flagged for high fraud, and your card will be blocked on the first attempt if you haven't set a travel notice.

### Digital Banks for Travelers
If you want to save on exchange fees, consider opening a **Wise** (formerly TransferWise) or **Nomad** account.
- **Nomad**: This is a digital bank specifically for Brazilians and travelers that provides a US Dollar account and a debit card that works seamlessly in Brazil with excellent exchange rates (mid-market rate + 1-2%).
- **Wise**: Their multi-currency card is highly reliable in Brazil and offers some of the lowest conversion fees available.

### Tipping Etiquette
Tipping is not as aggressive as in the US.
- **Restaurants**: A **10% or 12% service charge** is almost always included in the bill (*serviço*). You are not expected to tip more, though rounding up is appreciated for great service.
- **Taxis**: No tip required, but rounding up the fare is standard.
- **Hotel Porters**: R$5–10 per bag is appropriate.
