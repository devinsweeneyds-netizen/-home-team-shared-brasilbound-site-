# Connectivity: Staying Online in Brazil

Staying connected is not just a luxury in Brazil; it is a safety requirement. You will need data for Uber, Google Maps, and most importantly, WhatsApp. Here is how to ensure you never lose your signal.

### The eSIM Solution (Highly Recommended)
For most American travelers with modern iPhones or Pixels, an **eSIM** is the most frictionless option. 
- **Providers**: **Airalo** and **Holafly** are the leaders here. Airalo’s "Eurolink" doesn't apply, but their "Baobab" plan for Brazil is very reliable.
- **Pros**: You can buy and install the plan before you even leave the US. You’ll have data the moment your plane touches the tarmac.
- **Cons**: It is usually data-only (no local phone number).

### Local SIM Cards & The "CPF" Hurdle
If you want a local phone number (useful for some app registrations), you’ll need a physical SIM from one of the "Big Three": **Vivo**, **Claro**, or **TIM**.
- **The Challenge**: Technically, Brazilian law requires a **CPF** (a Brazilian tax ID) to register a SIM card. 
- **The Tourist Workaround**: Go to a major brand store in a shopping mall (not a street kiosk). Bring your passport. Ask for a "SIM Card para estrangeiros." Vivo and TIM usually have a process to register you using your passport number instead of a CPF, but it can be hit-or-miss depending on the clerk's training.
- **Cost**: Local SIMs are very cheap—roughly R$50 ($10 USD) will get you 10-20GB of data.

### WhatsApp: The Essential App
In Brazil, **WhatsApp is the internet.** 
- Restaurants use it for reservations.
- Hotels use it for guest services.
- Tour guides use it for coordination.
- **Tip**: Even if you have a US number, your WhatsApp will continue to work on your data plan. Make sure you have it installed and verified before you leave the US.

### WiFi (Wi-Fi)
- **Public WiFi**: Available in airports, major shopping malls, and Starbucks.
- **The "Local Number" Trap**: Many public WiFi networks (including airport ones) require you to receive a validation code via SMS to a Brazilian mobile number. This is another reason why an eSIM or local SIM is superior to relying on WiFi.
- **Security**: As in the US, use a **VPN** (like NordVPN or ExpressVPN) when using public networks to protect your data.

### Power & Plugs
- **The Plug**: Brazil uses the **Type N** plug (three round pins in a triangle). It is unique to Brazil and South Africa. 
- **Adapters**: While many outlets also accept the European Type C (two round pins), your flat-bladed US plugs will not work without an adapter.
- **Voltage**: This is a trap! Some cities (like Rio and São Paulo) are **127V**, while others (like Brasília or parts of the Northeast) are **220V**. Most modern electronics (laptops, phone chargers) are "dual voltage," but check the label before plugging in a hair dryer or flat iron, or you may fry it.
